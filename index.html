Drawing preparation · QA

PDF Stamp Tool

Matches every assembly to its drawing and places a QR-coded QA stamp in free space on each sheet.

How it works

  1. Drawings and the PowerFab report go in. Inside the Production Control Interface, both come straight from PowerFab.

  2. Files are renamed and matched to assembly marks. Missing drawings are listed.

  3. The tool finds an empty area on each sheet and repeats the stamp by quantity.

  4. Each QA stamp carries a QR code. A full sheet gets a blank page for its stamps.

  5. Out come the stamped set, the tag file and instance numbers.

QR sign-off on the shop floor

  1. The weld inspector finishes the dimensional check on the assembly.

  2. They scan the QR code on the drawing stamp with the Transportation App.

  3. PowerFab records the finish station straight away, not at the end of the day.

CNC programming · Beamline

3D Batch Viewer

Sits between PowerFab and the beamline software. Batches are checked, fixed and split here before they reach the machines.

How it works

  1. Nested batches come out of PowerFab as batch and NC files.

  2. Every batch is checked for what the machine software would reject, then fixed in one click.

  3. Batches are divided between the saw machine and the laser machine.

  4. A 3D model of the real factory runs each bar and gives the job time.

  5. A clean set of batches goes to the machine software.

Built into the viewer · Estimating

NC Processing Quote

Estimates the production price for a quotation before the job is received. It reads the job’s NC files, lists every part and prices the work per operation.

Parts list and face view of each part.

Shop time and cost by operation. Rates are blurred.

Two templates compared with a sanity check. Prices are blurred.

How it works

  1. The NC files of the job go in. This happens at quoting time, before the job is won.

  2. Every part is listed with its section, length and weight, and a drawing of each face.

  3. Cuts, holes, slots, notches and part marks are counted from the NC data.

  4. Two templates price the job: a unit price per operation, and shop time per operation at an hourly rate.

  5. The two totals are set side by side, with a sanity check on the average rate.

  6. The quote goes out as a PDF and as the Excel estimating sheet.

Checking PowerFab

Ask the chatbot…Send

How it works

  1. A welder or supervisor asks in a communication app such as Telegram or WhatsApp, in plain words.

  2. The chatbot picks up the message and sends it to Claude.

  3. Claude, the AI model, works out which job, lot or mark is meant and calls the MCP connector.

  4. The connector I built asks the ERP through its API. It is read-only: it can look things up but never change them.

  5. The ERP system, Tekla PowerFab, returns the live data through the API.

  6. The data goes back the same way. Claude reviews it and writes the answer in two or three lines.

  7. The chatbot posts the answer in the communication app: what is finished, and which station each remaining assembly is at.

Training · Tekla PowerFab

PowerFab Training Website

A self-paced site that teaches Tekla PowerFab topic by topic, then lets staff practise on a simulator of the real screens.

BeforeA trainer for every session

The documents and exercises also had to be prepared each time.

AfterSimulated, with AI agent support

Staff learn and practise on the simulator, and the AI agent answers their questions.

How it works

  1. Ten modules, from Estimating to PowerFab Go, with one page per topic.

  2. A simulator of the PowerFab screens. Eight topic tasks, from importing a job to loads and cutting lists, each checked by itself.

  3. A quiz for every module, then timed final tests: 10 questions in 15 minutes.

  4. Everyone signs in. A Results tab and an admin view show progress and scores. A kiosk version runs on shared terminals.

  5. The Chatbot AI Agent supports the training as well: staff can ask it questions while they learn.
